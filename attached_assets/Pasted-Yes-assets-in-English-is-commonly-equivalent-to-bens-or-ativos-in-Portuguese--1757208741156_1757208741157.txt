### In Portuguese

* The Cambridge Dictionary translates **“asset”** into Portuguese as **“bem”**, **“ativo”**, **“propriedade”**, among others ([Cambridge Dictionary][1]).
* Collins Dictionary similarly lists translations **“bem”** for general property, **“bens”** for plural, and **“ativo”** in a business context ([Collins Dictionary][2]).
* Linguee examples show **“asset”** rendered as **“ativo”** or **“bem”** depending on context ([Linguee][3]).
* A broader lexical source also lists **“bens”**, **“ativos”**, and even **“patrimônio”** depending on nuance ([WordHippo][4], [Reverso Context][5]).

In short:

* **“Asset” → “bem”** when referring to a valuable item or property.
* **“Asset” → “ativo”** in financial or accounting contexts (e.g., fixed asset).

### In Tetum

* A glossary relating land law defines **“rikusoin”** as:
  *“Rikusoin \<Bens/ Asset/ Aset> — Buat ruma ne’ebé ema posui no iha valór (rai, ekipamentu, osan, nst.).”*
  Which means: "Something that someone owns and that has value (land, equipment, money, etc.)" ([laohamutuk.org][6]).
* Another glossary (Standard Tetum–English) similarly equates **“Aset”** (Tetum variant) with **“Bens/ Asset/ Rikusoinn”**, defining it as: *"Hal atau sesuatu yang dimiliki dan bernilai (harta benda, perlengkapan, uang, hak cipta, pengetahuan...)"* — meaning "Thing or something owned and valuable (property, equipment, money, copyright, knowledge...)" ([laohamutuk.org][7]).

So in Tetum, **“rikusoin”** (or **“aset”**) is used as the direct counterpart of the English “asset,” just as *“bens/ ativos”* are in Portuguese.
